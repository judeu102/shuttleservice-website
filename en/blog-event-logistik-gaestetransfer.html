Event Logistics for Corporate Events: Planning Guest Transfer Properly

Planning · 10 min read

When planning a corporate event, most attention goes to the venue, the programme and the catering. Guest transfer is often only considered at the last minute, yet it frequently determines the first and last impression the event leaves. This guide shows how to approach transport planning in a structured way, depending on the type of event.

Why Transport Logistics Is Often Underestimated

One reason is that transport is rarely an issue at smaller internal meetings – everyone makes their own way. At corporate events with external guests, several hotels or international attendees, that changes abruptly. Suddenly arrival times have to be coordinated, capacities planned and, if in doubt, several vehicles organised at once. Anyone who only tackles this a week beforehand quickly ends up under time pressure.

The Planning Phase: What Must Be Clarified First

Three questions should be at the start of any planning:

  • How many guests are being transported, and are they arriving individually, in groups or at a fixed time?
  • How many locations are involved – hotel, airport and event venue, for instance?
  • How tight is the schedule, particularly for agenda items that depend on the punctual arrival of specific people?

The answers determine whether a single vehicle is enough or whether a coordinated fleet with a dedicated point of contact is needed. The Event Shuttle Service page provides the basics on organising such rides.

Trade Fairs and Exhibitions

At trade fairs, the biggest challenge is usually the fixed opening time: stand personnel have to be there before the fair opens, and important business partners are often collected directly from the airport. Since trade fairs run over several days, booking the same driver for the entire period is worthwhile – it saves repeated coordination. Details on this, including the transport of exhibition materials, can be found on the Trade Fairs & Exhibitions page.

Corporate Parties and Gala Evenings

At evening events, the first impression on arrival counts just as much as a safe journey home at the end – often after midnight. When many guests leave at different times, a staggered plan with several vehicles is needed. Executives are frequently transported separately and discreetly. More on this on the Corporate Parties & Galas page.

Congresses and Conferences

An additional factor comes into play here: speakers have to be on stage on time, often after an international flight. A sufficient time buffer between landing and appearance is crucial, as is a dedicated point of contact who coordinates short-notice schedule changes directly with the event management. Between several partner hotels and the venue, a fixed shuttle rotation is advisable. Described in detail on the Congresses & Conferences page.

Large Events

For events with a high volume of guests – concerts or large corporate events with several hundred attendees, say – a single vehicle is no longer enough. What matters here is coordination through a central point of contact, staggered arrivals and departures so that no backlog builds up at the entrances, and separate, discreet rides for artists or special guests. Details on fleet coordination on the Large Events page.

Choosing the Vehicle by Number of Guests

As a rough guide: for individual business partners or speakers, First Class with an S-Class or Audi A8 is a good fit. For groups of up to six people with luggage, the V-Class is the most practical choice. For very large groups, a combination of several vehicles is usually coordinated, matched to the actual arrival pattern of the guests. The full overview with passenger count and features for each class is on the Fleet page.

Checklist for Event Planning

  1. Determine the number of guests and arrival pattern (individual, grouped, at a fixed time)
  2. Define the locations: hotel, airport, venue and any intermediate stops
  3. Identify time-critical agenda items, such as speakers or guests of honour
  4. For multi-day events: request a fixed driver for the entire duration
  5. For high guest volumes: allow for a central point of contact for fleet coordination
  6. Plan separate rides for executives, speakers or VIP guests if required

Conclusion

Good event logistics barely get noticed, precisely because they work. By clarifying the guests’ arrival pattern early and choosing vehicles to match the type of event, you avoid the typical problems such as queues at the entrance or missed agenda items caused by delayed speakers.
